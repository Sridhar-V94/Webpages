What to keep in mind

How this comparison works & what it leaves out
Both scenarios start with the same available cash: the renter invests the down payment and the buyer's upfront stamp duty and registration costs. Each month the renter also invests the difference between the buyer's EMI, maintenance and property tax (less any buyer tax benefit entered) and the rent paid (less any renter tax benefit). If rent costs more, the model draws that difference from the portfolio, but never below zero. This keeps the cash-flow comparison like-for-like.
  • EMI uses a standard monthly reducing-balance loan; the rate is held constant and tenure cannot exceed the selected term. Each year's interest and principal are shown in the table.
  • Rent, maintenance and property tax grow at their entered inflation rates. Property value grows at the assumed annual appreciation rate.
  • The renter's blended return is the allocation-weighted average of the three entered asset returns. It is compounded monthly and rebalanced implicitly; actual portfolios vary and carry market risk.
  • Portfolio returns compound monthly. Buy net worth is estimated resale value less outstanding loan and the assumed selling-cost percentage. It is not a guaranteed sale price.
  • All results are nominal future rupees. The dashboard also estimates today's purchasing power by dividing net worth by (1 + general inflation)^years. Actual household inflation may differ.
  • Entered annual tax benefits are held flat in nominal rupees; they are estimates, not tax calculations.
  • Not modeled: slab-specific income-tax treatment or HRA eligibility beyond your manual estimates, capital-gains tax on either portfolio or the home sale, step-up/variable loan rates, vacancy, major repairs, brokerage on rent, furnishing, opportunity cost of time, and emotional or lifestyle value. State-level stamp duty varies.
Use a conservative range for returns and appreciation, and check tax and transaction rules with a qualified Indian professional before deciding.

Illustrative planning tool, not investment, tax, loan, or legal advice. Mutual funds and equity investments are subject to market risk. Actual costs, returns, taxes and property values can differ substantially.
